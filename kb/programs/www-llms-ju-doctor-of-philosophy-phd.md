FAQ's:
Q1. What is the duration of a PhD?
A1: A PhD or Doctor of Philosophy programme typically takes three to six years to complete, depending on whether it is full-time or part-time and the institution's policies.

Q2. Who is eligible for a PhD?
A2: To be eligible for a PhD, students must have a Master's degree in a relevant field with at least 55% marks. However, as per recent University Grants Commission (UGC) guidelines, students can apply after completing a four-year undergraduate degree. They must score at least 75% in graduation and clear the entrance examination.

Q3. Why is a PhD called a Doctor of Philosophy?
A3: The term "Doctor of Philosophy" (PhD) is used because the degree is a challenging and esteemed achievement representing a love of wisdom and knowledge. The term "doctor" recognises the degree holder's expertise in applying knowledge to solve real-world problems.

Q4. Is a PhD higher than a Master's?
A4: Yes. A PhD is considered a higher degree than a Master's; a PhD is the highest level of academic degree, while a Master's is regarded as the second level after a Bachelor's degree.

Q5. Can I complete my PhD in 2 years?
A5: Yes. It's possible to complete a PhD in two years, but it's rare and impressive. The time it takes to complete a PhD depends on several factors, including the student's research area, the institute they are studying at, and their academic performance.

Q6. Can I do a PhD without a Master's?
A6: Yes. According to the new UGC guidelines, students can now pursue PhD without completing their postgraduate studies, provided they score a minimum of 75% in graduation and clear the entrance examination.
