FAQ's:
Q1. What is the difference between a lawyer and an advocate?
A1: A lawyer can provide legal advice and draft documents, while an advocate represents clients in court. Both lawyers and advocates are legal professionals who are knowledgeable about laws and regulations.

Q2. What is LLM in law?
A2: LLM stands for Master of Laws, a postgraduate law degree that helps lawyers specialise in a particular area of law.

Q3. Is LLM hard?
A3: Yes. A LLM programme can be challenging, but it can also be rewarding. It requires hard work, critical thinking, effective study routine and good time management.

Q4. What is the qualification for LLM?
A4: LLM is a postgraduate qualification pursued after the completion of a bachelor's degree in law.

Q5. Who is eligible for LLM?
A5: To be eligible for an LLM degree, students need to have a bachelor's degree in law from a recognised university or college with a minimum of 55% aggregate marks.

Q6. Is LLM 1 year or 2 years?
A6: A Master of Laws (LLM) programme is usually two years long, but some universities offer a one-year programme. Students must check and confirm with the concerned authorities of the institution they are applying to before enrolling for the programme.

Q7. What is the LLB salary?
A7: The average salary for LLB graduates in India ranges between INR 3 to 8 Lacs per annum.

Q8. Is LLB compulsory for LLM?
A8: Yes. LLB, or Bachelor of Laws, is compulsory to apply for an LLM degree.
