FAQ's:
Q1. Who is the father of Bharatanatyam?
A1. Lord Brahma is said to have revealed Bharatanatyam to the sage Bharata, who then codified it in the Natya Shastra.

Q2. How many years for Arangetram?
A2. It can take several years of training to perform an Arangetram, a Bharatanatyam debut performance. The time it takes depends on the individual's practice and the dancer's teacher.

Q3. What does MPA stand for?
A3. MPA stands for Master of Performing Arts in Bharatanatyam. It's a two-year postgraduate programme that focuses on the theory and practice of Bharatanatyam.

Q4. What are the 7 stages of Bharatanatyam?
A4. The seven stages of a Bharatanatyam recital are Alarippu, Jatiswaram, Varnam, Padams, Tillana, Shloka, and the audience's response.

Q5. What next after Arangetram?
A5. After an Arangetram, a dancer can continue learning Bharatanatyam by expanding their repertoire, learning choreography, and studying theory.

Q6. Who is the queen of Bharatanatyam?
A6. Tanjore Balasaraswati is known as the queen of Bharatanatyam. She was an Indian dancer who popularised the classical dance style in India and abroad.

Q7. Can I learn Bharatanatyam at 50?
A7. Yes. One can learn Bharatanatyam at age 50 or older. It's never too late to start learning Bharatanatyam, an ancient dance form transcending age barriers. With dedication, guidance, and a positive mindset, anyone can learn Bharatanatyam at any age.

Q8. How long is the Bharatanatyam course?
A8: A Bharatanatyam course usually takes several years to complete, with most dancers needing around 5-8 years of consistent practice to reach proficiency. However, pursuing a Master of Performing Arts in Bharatanatyam usually takes two years.
