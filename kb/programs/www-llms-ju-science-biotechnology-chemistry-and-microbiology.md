FAQ's:
Q1. Is a Microbiologist a doctor?
A1. No. A microbiologist is not usually a doctor, but a medical microbiologist is a doctor who specialises in microbiology. They work in pathology and may also contribute to public health strategies.

Q2. Does Biotechnology need NEET?
A2. No. NEET is not required for admission to undergraduate biotechnology courses. NEET is needed for admissions into medical and dental courses in India. Students may need to clear JEE or JEE Advanced if they are enrolling for a BTech course in IIT or any other engineering college.

Q3. Is Biotechnology in high demand?
A3. Yes. Biotechnology is a growing field with many opportunities in healthcare, agriculture, environmental science, and industrial biotechnology.

Q4. Does Biotechnology have a future?
A4. Yes. Biotechnology is expected to impact human life and health constructively in the future. India's biotechnology industry is among the largest biotechnology industries in Asia and is growing at a rapid pace.

Q5. What is BSc in Chemistry?
A5. A Bachelor's in Science awarded in Chemistry is an undergraduate programme that explains the nature of matter in detail. It includes subdivisions such as organic, inorganic, physical chemistry, analytical chemistry and biochemistry.

Q6. Is BSc Chemistry easy?
A6. BSc Chemistry is generally not considered "easy" due to its complex concepts. The coursework demands extensive laboratory work, along with a rigorous theory curriculum that requires dedication and interest to succeed.

Q7. Can I join ISRO after BSc Chemistry?
A7. To join ISRO, students can apply for different technical and scientific positions advertised by ISRO through their recruitment portal.

Q8. Is BSc Chemistry a good career?
A8. Yes, a BSc in Chemistry can be a good career choice, offering diverse opportunities in research, pharmaceuticals, agro-industry, environmental science, food and other industrial applications.

Q9. Does BSc Chemistry require Maths?
A9. Yes. Maths is a compulsory subject in the syllabus of BSc Chemistry.

Q10. Who is the father of Microbiology?
A10. Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, a Dutch scientist and businessman, is known as the father of microbiology.

Q11. What is BSc Microbiology?
A10. A Bachelor of Science (BSc) in Microbiology is a three-year undergraduate degree that studies microorganisms and their impact on humans and the environment.

Q12. Does Microbiology require NEET?
A12. No. Students do not need the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) to pursue a course in microbiology in India. Instead, admission is based on merit and university-specific entrance exam scores (if any).

Q13. Is Microbiology a good career?
A13. Yes. The need for knowledge is increasing in the life sciences about antibiotic resistance, environmental cleanup, and infectious disease control. Hence, the field of microbiology is gaining more and more job opportunities.

Q14. Is Microbiology hard to study?
A14. Microbiology can be a rigorous course as it involves complex concepts in biochemistry, genetics, and immunology. However, with effective time management and a high level of dedication, students can easily ace the programme.

Q15. Can I do MD after BSc Microbiology?
A15. No. Students cannot directly pursue a Doctor of Medicine (MD) after completing a BSc in Microbiology. To do an MD, they must first complete a Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) degree, which is the standard medical prerequisite for further specialisation through an MD programme.

Q16. Can I get a job after BSc Microbiology?
A16. Yes. Students can find several career opportunities in fields like healthcare, research, the food industry, environmental science, and biotechnology. They can work as clinical microbiologists, research laboratory technicians, food microbiologists, quality control analysts, and environmental microbiologists.

Q17. Can I join ISRO after BSc Microbiology?
A17. Yes. Students can join ISRO after completing a BSc in Microbiology from a recognised college or university with above 60% marks. However, pursuing a master's or PhD degree can increase the chances of pursuing a substantial research-oriented position within the organisation.
